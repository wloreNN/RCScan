# M5 Findings and Risk Prioritization

## Purpose and boundary

M5 converts eligible M4 vulnerability-intelligence candidates into structured,
explainable findings. It uses only evidence already held in the completed scan; it
does not make additional network requests, exploit a target, execute a proof of
concept, verify a vulnerability, or validate exploitability.

A **candidate** is an M4 `POTENTIAL_MATCH`: a provider record correlated with an
observed and normalized product/version. A **finding** is the M5 representation of an
eligible candidate for one open service endpoint, with effective applicability,
derived severity, evidence confidence, deterministic priority, bounded evidence,
references, and remediation provenance. A finding remains potential. It is not proof
that the endpoint is vulnerable, exploitable, or safe.

Active verification is a separate, default-off, explicitly registered boundary.
Findings and verification results remain distinct records. JSON, HTML, and SARIF
reports are generated by the reporting stage. There is no findings database,
dashboard, or workflow.

## Eligibility and effective applicability

Finding generation runs after optional vulnerability enrichment. It requires an open
TCP port, a non-`UNKNOWN` fingerprint, a supported normalized product/version, and a
valid vulnerability candidate.

Effective applicability combines the candidate's affected-version result with its
environment applicability:

- If either value is `NO_MATCH`, no finding is emitted.
- Otherwise, if either value is `INDETERMINATE`, effective applicability is
  `INDETERMINATE`.
- Only `MATCH` plus `MATCH` produces effective applicability `MATCH`.
- Any unsupported combination is omitted.

This effective applicability is not deployment verification. M4 does not establish
operating system, architecture, enabled modules, vulnerable feature use, deployment
role, compensating controls, vulnerable-code reachability, or exploit prerequisites.

## Published CVSS and derived severity

Published CVSS is provider metadata copied from the selected candidate metric.
RCScan does not calculate or alter a CVSS score. M5 separately derives its own
severity label from the published base score using these exact bands:

- Missing CVSS: `UNKNOWN` derived severity.
- `0.0`: `INFORMATIONAL`.
- Greater than `0.0` and less than `4.0`: `LOW`.
- `4.0` through less than `7.0`: `MEDIUM`.
- `7.0` through less than `9.0`: `HIGH`.
- `9.0` through `10.0`: `CRITICAL`.

The provider's published base-severity text may be displayed beside the published
score, but the deterministic priority policy uses the derived severity above. Missing
CVSS therefore produces informational priority; no score is invented.

## Confidence and deterministic priority

Finding confidence is the M4 candidate's evidence confidence
(`match_confidence`), not CVSS and not a probability of exploitation. It describes
the strength of the product/version correlation and whether published ranges and
environment conditions could be resolved. It is retained as `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, or
`LOW`, together with a bounded explanation.

Priority starts at the derived severity and applies this exact policy:

- Effective `NO_MATCH`: no finding.
- Effective `MATCH` with `HIGH` confidence: keep the derived severity level.
- Effective `MATCH` with `MEDIUM` confidence: lower one level.
- Effective `INDETERMINATE` with `HIGH` or `MEDIUM` confidence: lower one level.
- `LOW` confidence, for either retained applicability: lower two levels.
- Reductions stop at `INFORMATIONAL`; priority never falls below that floor.
- Missing CVSS (`UNKNOWN` severity) maps directly to `INFORMATIONAL` priority.

The ordered levels are `CRITICAL`, `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`, and `INFORMATIONAL`.
Priority is deterministic triage, not a verified business-risk calculation. It does
not include asset criticality, threat activity, internet exposure, compensating
controls, or organization-specific impact.

## Stable identity and deduplication

Finding IDs have the form `AF-` followed by the first 24 hexadecimal characters of a
SHA-256 digest. The normalized logical identity includes host, TCP port, service,
normalized vendor, normalized product, and upper-cased CVE ID. It intentionally does
not include timestamps, provider cache/live provenance, description, CVSS, priority,
or version, so the same logical endpoint/CVE identity remains stable when those
details change.

Within one scan result, duplicate finding IDs are removed in host/port/candidate
traversal order and the first finding is retained. This is deterministic
deduplication, not cross-scan persistence or lifecycle tracking.

## Bounded evidence, references, and output

Each finding contains one generated observation summary, up to five bounded
fingerprint evidence summaries, one normalized-identity summary, and one correlation
summary. Individual finding-evidence summaries are limited to 500 characters.
Descriptions are limited to 2,000 characters.

References retain at most ten unique, valid HTTP(S) URLs in provider order, each
limited to 500 characters. In verbose CLI output, at most three references per
displayed finding are printed and the additional count is reported. Metadata retains
at most 20 string entries, with keys limited to 100 characters and values to 500;
obvious secret-key names are excluded.

`findings.max_displayed` bounds complete-scan CLI finding output. Its default is 20
and its accepted range is 1–100; hidden findings are counted. Findings remain bounded
in-memory records and are not written to a report or database.

## Remediation provenance

Provider remediation is used only when candidate metadata supplies both non-empty
`remediation` and `remediation_source` strings. It is normalized and labelled
`PROVIDER`, preserving the supplied source. Guidance is limited to 1,500 characters
and its source to 200 characters.

Otherwise M5 supplies conservative generic update-and-exposure guidance labelled
`GENERIC` with source `RCScan generic guidance`. The current NVD integration does
not synthesize provider remediation from ordinary references, so generic guidance is
the normal fallback. Neither form proves applicability or successful remediation;
operators must confirm against the vendor advisory and their deployment.

## Failure isolation

Malformed or ineligible candidates are skipped independently. A duplicate is skipped
without affecting its original finding. If whole finding generation unexpectedly
fails, `ScanRunner` logs debug detail, preserves all scan and candidate evidence,
returns no generated findings, and still completes the otherwise successful scan.
Finding generation never turns optional enrichment failure into scan failure.

## M6 relationship to findings

Active verification runs after this M5 policy only when enabled by
`--active-verification` or `verification.enabled: true`. It never recalculates or
mutates a finding's CVSS, severity, applicability, confidence, or priority, regardless
of verification status.

The M6 registry ships only the exact synthetic `CVE-DEMO-M6-0001` rule. Normal
NVD-derived findings have no rule, so they receive `SKIPPED` with zero requests and
no generic fallback probe. `VERIFIED` means only that a reviewed synthetic marker was
observed, not that the finding is exploitable. `NOT_VERIFIED` and every other
non-verified outcome are not proof that the service is safe. See
[active-verification.md](active-verification.md).

Vulnerability lookup is still disabled by default
(`vulnerability.enabled: false`), while finding generation is enabled by default
(`findings.enabled: true`). Therefore the default pipeline normally has no candidates
from which to generate findings. Disabling findings suppresses M5 generation but does
not remove M4 candidates.

## False positives and false negatives

False positives remain possible because banners can be misleading, product versions
can be backported or vendor-patched, provider records can be incomplete or stale,
range correlation does not prove vulnerable-code reachability, and environment
conditions or compensating controls may be unresolved. A high-priority finding still
requires deployment-specific review.

False negatives remain possible because fingerprinting and lookup are bounded,
unsupported products or non-dotted versions are not normalized, hidden or absent
version evidence cannot be correlated, lookup is off by default, provider/cache
failure is isolated, M4 limits retained candidates, and malformed candidates are
skipped. `NO_MATCH` and absence of findings do not prove that a service is secure.

## Offline acceptance

The parent-provided deterministic M5 demonstration runs without a provider:

```text
python scripts/m5_findings_demo.py
```

Acceptance expects the script to complete successfully and demonstrate deterministic
policy output for `CVE-DEMO-0001` as CVSS 9.8 CRITICAL, `MATCH`, `HIGH` confidence,
and `CRITICAL` priority. It also shows `CVE-DEMO-0002` as `INDETERMINATE` with
`MEDIUM` confidence and reduced `HIGH` priority. Both identifiers are explicitly
synthetic and must not be interpreted as real vulnerabilities or verification
results.
